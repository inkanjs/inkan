# 0.9.0: the same speed, checked

0.9 has no performance work in it. What it adds (`npm create inkan`, `app.upgrade`, the CLI
formats, `untested`) is off the request path, except that `listen` now runs its WebSockets
through the same `accept` as `app.upgrade`. So the bench is here to show that nothing got
slower, not to find points.

## On the GitHub runner, release gate

2026-11-03, `release-0.9` against v0.8.1 as `inkan-base`, all servers at once, plus

This runner was a little slower for everyone than the one of the 0.8.0 bench (Fastify 87.2
against 89.9, inkan 0.8.1 87.4 against 91.0 for 0.8.0), so the numbers are for comparing
within this table, not with the last one.

The gate:

1. **inkan above Fastify: in every run, not outside the spread.** 89.4 against 87.2 on the
   median, ahead in all three runs (by 2.0, 2.2 and 2.5 points), but the ranges touch
   (87.5–91.0 against 85.0–89.0).
2. **inkan above Fastify in every scenario: not met.** Fastify leads hello at 10
   connections (95 against 91), the big JSON body (91 against 86), the async handler (96
   against 92), 3 middlewares (91 against 88) and params + query by a little (91 against 89);
   inkan leads the big answer, the 400, the small body, the 404 and hello at 100 and 512.
3. **No scenario worse than 0.8.1: met.** 89.4 against 87.4 on the median, ahead in two
   runs of three; no scenario is lower than 0.8.1 by more than its spread (the big JSON body,
   86 against 88, has 86–96 against 88–90).

Request ids cost nothing measurable this time: inkan-noid 89.1 against 89.4 (0.8.0 had 2
points between them).

So 0.9.0 is as fast as 0.8, and ahead of Fastify on this bench in every run, by about two
points, though not in every scenario.
